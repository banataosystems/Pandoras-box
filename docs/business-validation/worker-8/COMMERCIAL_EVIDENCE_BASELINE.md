## 7. Problem-discovery interview

### 7.1 Initial sample

Target the first **10 qualified interviews** in the selected wedge. This is an evidence threshold, not a promise that ten interviews prove the market.

A qualified participant:

- is the buyer, daily process owner, or direct user;
- handled the workflow within the prior 30 days;
- can describe a real recent case;
- can explain the decision and budget process;
- is not counted twice for the same business and decision unit.

Do not show a product demo during the first twenty minutes. Do not ask “Would you use an AI app builder?”

### 7.2 Script

1. “Walk me through the last customer inquiry that became a completed job.”
2. “Where did the inquiry arrive, and who saw it first?”
3. “What information had to be collected before you could accept or schedule it?”
4. “What happened the last time an inquiry was delayed, lost, duplicated, or misunderstood?”
5. “How often does that happen in a normal week or month?”
6. “What does a failed or delayed job cost: revenue, staff time, customer trust, or rework?”
7. “What tools, notebooks, chats, spreadsheets, or software do you use today?”
8. “What have you already tried, and why did it not stick?”
9. “Who would need to approve a change? Who would use it every day?”
10. “What budget has been spent on this problem before?”
11. “What would need to be true for you to commit staff time to a pilot?”
12. “What would make you stop using a new system after the first month?”
13. “What outcome would make you renew or add a second workflow?”
14. “May I summarize what I heard and ask whether it is accurate?”

### 7.3 Evidence capture

Store a structured summary only:

`segment, role, problem, frequency, current_solution, current_cost, severity, urgency, alternatives_tried, decision_process, budget_signal, willingness_to_pay_signal, objections, desired_outcome, follow_up`

Use a pseudonymous customer code. Do not store raw private conversations, personal identifiers, secrets, customer data, or confidential workflow records in generic Memory.

### 7.4 Signal classification

| Level | Required behavior |
|---|---|
| **Weak** | Compliment, hypothetical interest, link request, free signup |
| **Moderate** | Shares real workflow, commits implementation time, introduces decision maker, agrees to detailed pilot discussion |
| **Strong / paid** | Accepts a scoped price, requests approved procurement/invoice process, pays, renews, or expands |

## 8. First paid-pilot design

### 8.1 Scope

- one independent business;
- one location;
- one inquiry-to-completion workflow;
- one owner/approver and up to five operating users;
- one bounded intake method;
- owner-visible queue, scheduling/status, completion record, and basic outcome report;
- 30 live measurement days after activation;
- no payment processing, SMS purchase, public claim, regulated action, broad CRM/ERP replacement, or unsupported integration;
- all onboarding, support, debugging, retry, fallback, and custom work metered.

### 8.2 Activation

**Activated customer:** the first eligible real inquiry reaches a confirmed scheduled job and appears with correct status in the owner's live queue without developer intervention during that journey.

Account creation, login, prompt submission, project creation, or deployment alone is not activation.

### 8.3 Successful customer outcome

An eligible real inquiry reaches the agreed terminal state—confirmed and scheduled, explicitly declined, or closed with a recorded reason—with correct ownership, status, and audit evidence, no critical failure, and no unrecorded manual rescue.

### 8.4 Proposed acceptance criteria

Final thresholds must be agreed only after the customer's baseline is measured. The initial test design is:

- at least 20 eligible real inquiries, or an agreed lower-volume full cohort;
- at least 80% of eligible inquiries captured, with exclusions recorded;
- a measurable baseline-to-pilot improvement in median time to confirmed/declined status;
- correct terminal status in at least 90% of a sampled audit;
- owner performs at least one approved operating update from a common Android phone without a developer;
- zero critical security, privacy, tenant-isolation, data-loss, or authorization incident;
- every failed outcome, retry, fallback, and manual rescue recorded;
- customer confirms whether the system should continue after the live window.

No ROI amount is claimed before real baseline and outcome data exist.

### 8.5 Support boundary

The pilot tests Pandora, not unlimited bespoke consulting.

Track:

- onboarding minutes;
- support interactions and minutes;
- custom configuration;
- integration work;
- debugging and implementation rescue;
- founder intervention;
- customer training;
- reason each intervention was required.

Extensive custom engineering means services evidence, not yet scalable Pandora evidence.

## 9. Three pricing hypotheses

All prices are **untested hypotheses in Philippine pesos**. No quote, invoice, charge, or contract has been sent.

### P1 — Fixed outcome pilot

**PHP 15,000 once** for the bounded pilot scope and 30 live measurement days.

Purpose: simplest comprehension and willingness-to-pay test. Third-party messaging, payment, or other paid services are excluded unless separately authorized.

### P2 — Setup plus persistent access

**PHP 7,500 setup + PHP 4,500/month** for one live workflow, one location, and the agreed usage/support ceiling.

Purpose: tests whether the buyer distinguishes implementation from continuing value.

### P3 — Subscription plus Build and Runtime Credits

**PHP 3,000/month access + PHP 8,000 initial Build Credit pack. Runtime Credits remain unpriced until direct usage cost is measured.**

Purpose: tests long-term architecture and customer comprehension. Do not externally quote a Runtime Credit price before actual inference, compute, API, message, and support cost evidence exists.

### Price-comprehension test

Ask the prospect to explain:

- subscription = persistent access and ongoing product value;
- Build Credits = creating, changing, reviewing, testing, or repairing the system;
- Runtime Credits = operating workflows, integrations, compute, messages, APIs, and inference.

If the buyer cannot explain the model accurately, use the fixed outcome package rather than adding billing complexity.

### Price-change rule

After **five qualified buyers** receive the same scoped offer:

- zero acceptance and zero serious counteroffers: revisit problem, value, scope, and then price;
- repeated “too expensive” with strong behavioral commitment: test a narrower outcome before discounting;
- fast acceptance without negotiation plus high realized value: test a higher price with the next comparable cohort;
- do not infer repeatable pricing from one payment.

## 10. Business-model and unit-economics contract

### 10.1 Revenue layers

1. **Subscription:** persistent access and continuing value.
2. **Build Credits:** variable creation/change/repair work.
3. **Runtime Credits:** variable operation and third-party usage.
4. **Enterprise:** deferred until repeated independent demand and economically justified requirements exist.

### 10.2 Variable COGS

Record as variable COGS when attributable to customer usage:

- model inference and agent execution;
- retries, fallbacks, and repair generation;
- runtime compute and hosting;
- third-party APIs and integrations;
- email, SMS, media, and scheduled work;
- variable onboarding and support labor;
- debugging and manual rescue.

Founder labor receives a measured shadow cost even if no cash wage is paid.

### 10.3 Formulas

`total_revenue = subscription_revenue + build_revenue + runtime_revenue`

`variable_support_cost = support_minutes / 60 × loaded_support_hourly_rate`

`outcome_contribution = total_revenue − inference_cost − runtime_cost − third_party_api_cost − variable_support_cost`

`gross_profit_per_successful_outcome = outcome_contribution / successful_outcomes`

When successful outcomes equal zero, gross profit per successful outcome is undefined, not zero.

## 11. Retention, expansion, churn, and cohorts

### D30

Evaluate in the window from day 23 through day 37 after activation.

Stronger qualifying evidence:

- the system completes at least one real workflow outcome;
- the customer remains active and has not canceled;
- continued payment or an approved renewal decision is recorded;
- support/rescue burden remains measured.

Dashboard login alone does not qualify.

### D90

Evaluate in the window from day 83 through day 97 after activation using the same outcome-based rule. Record renewal, active runtime, repeat builds, new workflows, or expansion separately.

### Expansion

Strong signals include:

- second workflow;
- additional location or team;
- more runtime;
- repeat build/change request;
- paid higher package;
- owner referral tied to a successful result.

### Churn reason

Use one primary classification and preserve detail:

`problem_not_painful, solution_incomplete, poor_reliability, price, implementation_friction, support_burden, business_changed, competitor, one_time_use, unknown`

### Cohorts

Keep cohorts separate by:

- activation month;
- ICP;
- workflow;
- acquisition channel;
- price/package;
- technical-alpha generation;
- support intensity.

Do not calculate LTV from one pilot or arbitrary lifetime assumptions.
