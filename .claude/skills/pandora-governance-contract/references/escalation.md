# Escalation format

The owner is on a smartphone. An escalation that requires scrolling, or that ends without a clear question, wastes the interruption.

## Format

```
DECISION NEEDED: <one line, the actual choice>

WHY: <2-3 sentences — what you found, why it needs them>

RECOMMENDED: <your recommendation, stated plainly>

IF YES: <what you will do, and what it costs — money, risk, irreversibility>
IF NO:  <what happens instead, and what stays blocked>

ALREADY DONE: <safe work you completed while this was pending>
```

## Rules

**Always recommend.** "What would you like to do?" pushes analysis back onto the owner. You did the investigation; state your conclusion. They can override.

**Say the cost out loud.** New spending gets a number or a range. Irreversibility gets named as irreversible. Regulated activation gets named as regulated.

**Never escalate an empty-handed turn.** Before interrupting, finish everything that does not depend on the answer. The escalation should read "here is what I completed, and here is the one thing I cannot decide" — not "I stopped at the first uncertainty."

**One decision per escalation** where possible. Bundling three unrelated approvals into one message produces a partial answer and another round trip.

**Do not re-escalate a settled decision.** If the owner reaffirms a request after you raised a concern, that is their call. Proceed with the full request and say you are proceeding. Repeating the concern is not diligence; it is friction.

## Do not escalate for

- work that is safe, reversible, and costless
- something you can verify yourself with a read-only tool
- permission to look at provider state
- confirmation that a plan you already have authorization for is still wanted
- choices with an obvious default where you can state the assumption and proceed

## When blocked without an owner

If no human is present and the action requires authorization, stop that specific action, complete everything else in scope, and record the blocker precisely — what is needed, from whom, and what unblocks when it arrives. Do not degrade the action into a weaker one that avoids the gate. Substituting an unauthorized-but-smaller mutation for the one that needed approval defeats the gate.
